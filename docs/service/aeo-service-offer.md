**A ready-to-sell managed service for B2B SaaS companies**  
Prepared: 18 October 2026

## The offer in one sentence

We help B2B software companies become easier to discover, understand, cite, and recommend across AI-assisted search and traditional search—then report what changed, using a repeatable monthly program.

**Short promise:** Make your expertise easier for buyers and answer engines to find.  
**Do not promise:** guaranteed ChatGPT/Google AI citations, rankings, traffic, or revenue.

## Who to sell it to

Start with B2B SaaS companies that:

- Sell a considered purchase (roughly $5,000+ annual contract value, or a high-volume product with clear conversion tracking).
- Have an established website, at least a few subject experts or customers to interview, and a marketing owner who can approve changes.
- Already invest in content or SEO, but cannot tell whether AI answers describe them accurately or recommend competitors instead.

**Buyer:** VP/Head of Marketing, Demand Generation, Content, or SEO.  
**Good first verticals:** cybersecurity, HR/payroll software, finance operations, developer tools, and vertical SaaS. Pick one where you can access credible customer proof and subject experts.

**Not a fit yet:** brand-new businesses without product-market evidence, sites with major indexation problems and no SEO support, or teams expecting guaranteed placement from a one-time technical change.

## What the work includes

### 1. Baseline what buyers see

- Agree on products, ideal customers, competitors, geographies, and high-value decisions.
- Build a fixed set of realistic prompts: category discovery, problem diagnosis, comparisons, buying criteria, pricing, implementation, and product-specific questions.
- Record results by platform and date: whether the company appears, whether it is described correctly, whether a useful source is cited, which competitors appear, and what evidence supports the answer.
- Keep the prompt wording and collection method stable between reporting periods. Treat the results as a directional sample: answer outputs vary by platform, location, account state, and time.

### 2. Fix discoverability and trust gaps

- Check indexability, robots directives, canonical pages, page rendering, sitemap/internal links, and priority content availability.
- For ChatGPT search, confirm the site's policy on allowing **OAI-SearchBot** and explain the trade-off to the client. Do not change crawler access without client approval.
- Make essential product facts easy to verify: what it does, who it is for, supported integrations, pricing approach, implementation, limitations, security/compliance claims, and customer evidence.
- Correct outdated or conflicting information on owned pages and important public profiles.
- Review schema only where it accurately represents visible page content and supports a real search/use case.

### 3. Publish evidence-rich content

- Improve the pages buyers actually need: product/category pages, comparisons, alternatives, use cases, pricing, implementation, and expert answers.
- Use first-hand product knowledge, expert interviews, original data, named authors/reviewers, transparent methodology, and specific customer proof where available.
- Write to fully resolve the buyer's question, with clear claims, context, and links to primary evidence. Avoid thin mass-produced FAQs and copied summaries.
- Recommend relevant third-party coverage or profiles when there is a genuine editorial/business reason. Never buy fake reviews, fabricate mentions, or spam communities.

### 4. Measure outcomes buyers can use

Report three layers separately:

1. **Visibility signals:** mention rate, cited-source rate, answer accuracy, and share of voice within the agreed prompt sample.
2. **Search and site behavior:** Search Console performance, organic landing-page trends, referral visits (including ChatGPT referrals when available), engaged sessions, and conversions.
3. **Business evidence:** qualified leads or pipeline influenced by pages or referrals where analytics and CRM data support attribution.

Do not present prompt counts, AI “rankings,” or a single successful answer as proof of business impact. Separate observed results from interpretation and recommendations.

## Operating model and boundaries

- **Client provides:** product and claims source of truth; access to analytics/Search Console/CMS as needed; a subject expert; approval of factual/legal/security claims; one decision-maker; timely publishing support.
- **Provider owns:** research, prompt set, audit, recommendations, agreed content work, QA, reporting, and change log.
- **Publishing:** clarify whether the provider publishes directly or supplies drafts. Include direct implementation only when CMS access and client approval process are agreed.
- **Included limits:** packages cap prompts, platforms, pages, markets, and meetings as stated. Extra languages, domains, products, engineering work, PR outreach, and paid tools are separately scoped.
- **Reporting cadence:** baseline at start; monthly monitoring and report; formal 90-day review.
- **Cancellation:** after the initial minimum term, monthly renewal with 30 days' notice.
- **No guarantees:** third-party answer engines decide what to crawl, index, retrieve, cite, and display. The service improves readiness and measures observed visibility; it cannot control platform outputs.
